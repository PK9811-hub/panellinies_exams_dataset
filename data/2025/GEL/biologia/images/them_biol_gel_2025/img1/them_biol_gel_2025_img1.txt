Description
Figure 1: Illustration of the concentration of the enzyme $\beta$-galactosidase over time, $t$, showcasing the classic regulatory response of the lac operon in bacteria. The graph features two distinct curves starting from the origin, with a critical time marker $t_1$ indicated on the x-axis. Curve 1 demonstrates a significant, sigmoidal increase in enzyme concentration following time $t_1$, representing the induction of the operon, which typically occurs when lactose is added to the medium in the absence of glucose. In contrast, Curve 2 remains nearly horizontal and close to the baseline, indicating that the synthesis of $\beta$-galactosidase remains repressed or at very low basal levels, as would be expected if the inducer were absent or if a preferred carbon source like glucose were available. The vertical dotted line at $t_1$ specifically marks the point of induction where the environmental change triggers the transcription of the structural genes, leading to the rapid accumulation of the enzyme seen in the first curve.
