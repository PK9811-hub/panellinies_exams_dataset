Figure 2: A genealogical pedigree chart displays two generations, labeled with Roman numerals I and II, to track the inheritance of a specific trait. In the first generation, an affected male, represented by a black square (I-1), and an affected female, represented by a black circle (I-2), are joined by a horizontal mating line. Their offspring in the second generation include an unaffected female, indicated by a white circle (II-1), and a second individual marked by a black diamond (II-2), which signifies an affected offspring of unspecified sex. The appearance of an unaffected child from two affected parents strongly suggests that the trait is inherited in an autosomal dominant manner, as both parents must be heterozygous to produce a phenotypically normal daughter.
