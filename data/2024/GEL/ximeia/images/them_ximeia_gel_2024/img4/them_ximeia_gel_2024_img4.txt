Figure 4: A multi-step organic reaction scheme illustrating the transformation of an initial compound through several intermediates and reaction conditions. The starting compound, labeled A, has the general formula CₙH₂ᵥO and reacts with H₂ in the presence of Ni, forming compound B. Compound B is then treated with SOCl₂, producing compound Γ. The compound Γ reacts with Mg in absolute ether (απόλυτος αιθέρας), forming compound Δ. Compound Δ subsequently reacts with compound A, yielding an intermediate product (ενδιάμεσο προϊόν), which upon addition of H₂O forms compound E. From compound E, several reaction pathways are shown. In one pathway, treatment with KOH/Br₂ leads to the formation of compounds Λ and M. In another pathway, reaction with KMnO₄/H₂SO₄ produces compound Θ, which further reacts with NaHCO₃ to give compound K. Additionally, compound E can undergo dehydration in the presence of concentrated H₂SO₄ at 170°C, forming compound Z. The diagram is arranged with arrows indicating the direction of each reaction step, reaction conditions written above or beside the arrows, and each compound represented by a circled capital Greek letter.
